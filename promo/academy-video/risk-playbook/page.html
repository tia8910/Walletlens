    Why this style works

    What makes the 3D brick style work

    Drama first, lesson second

    The hook is a character screaming at a loss. The lesson arrives as the twist, so it never feels like a lecture.

    First-person storyteller

    "That's when the kid asked…" A friendly narrator voice tells the story as if it happened at their school.

    Two-line captions

    Three to five words at a time, white with a heavy black outline, sitting just below the faces. They're readable on mute.

    Faces carry everything

    Blocky bodies, but big swappable faces: scream, smug, cry, grin. Each cut changes an expression.

    A familiar world

    A school cafeteria with deep blue walls, shiny floors and party hats. It's instantly recognisable to a young audience.

    Re-hook every chapter

    Each chapter ends on a cliffhanger ("then the kid borrowed 10x…"), so viewers stay for the next rule.

    Production

    How to make the real video

    Option 1 · Roblox Studio

    Build the cafeteria and avatars in Roblox Studio and animate them with an animation plugin, the way story channels like the ones you shared do. This gives the exact look, and you can tag it #roblox.

    Option 2 · Blender

    Use the same blocky characters, with full control over lighting, the shiny floor and the camera. Render at 4K, 16:9.

    Option 3 · Code (this animatic)

    Grow this Three.js scene into a rendered video with Remotion in the repo. Fastest to iterate, but the least polished of the three.

    Brand safety: the kid, his friend and the classmates are original characters. If you build in Roblox Studio, follow Roblox's rules for videos and don't suggest Roblox endorses WalletLens. If you build in Blender or code, don't use the Roblox name or logo in the title or thumbnail.
